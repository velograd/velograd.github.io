01 / ABSTRACT

From terminal action values to local flow velocities.

Value-based RL for flow policies has to connect what a critic scores — the final action — to what the policy predicts — a sequence of local velocities.

Value-based reinforcement fine-tuning of flow policies must connect final-action values to the local velocities that generate them. We introduce VeloGrad, which learns this connection with a velocity-conditioned critic. The critic predicts the action value obtained by applying a candidate velocity for one flow step and completing generation under a frozen copy of the current policy. Since fitting values alone does not directly supervise the derivative used by the actor, we also train directional derivatives against normalized action-value differences from symmetric finite-radius velocity perturbations and their downstream continuations.

Actor updates use the learned derivative without backpropagating through the remaining flow solver; deployment uses the standard sampler without critic queries. Experiments cover offline and offline-to-online learning on OGBench and Robomimic. VeloGrad achieves 79.6% average offline success and 97.7% after online adaptation across four OGBench domains. On a Piper-X robot, it achieves 29/30 success on disturbed flower insertion and 21/30 autonomous end-to-end success on Windmill Activation, compared with 16/30 for DSRL.

03 / METHOD

A local interface between flow velocities and action values.

The critic scores completed actions. The policy predicts velocities. VeloGrad learns the missing map, then updates the actor through that map.

Local continuation value

For an intermediate flow state x, time t, and candidate velocity u, Qθ̄ is the action-critic value of taking one Euler step with u and completing the remaining flow under a frozen policy copy.

Qθ̄(s, x, t, u) = QA(s, Φt+h(t)→1(x + h(t) u))

Derivative supervision

Value matching alone does not supervise ∇uQv. Symmetric perturbations u ± εξ continue to terminal actions a+, a−. Their normalized value gap is the directional target.

D*ξ = [QA(s, a+) − QA(s, a−)] / 2ε

Actor update without BPTT

The actor maximizes the velocity critic at replay-anchored continuous flow states, plus a flow-matching regularizer. The remaining solver is never differentiated through.

Lactor = λFM LFM − λQ Q̄v(s, xtD, t, vθ)

Deployment

Evaluation uses the standard Euler flow sampler. Critics are training-only. Online real-robot adaptation uses a shared replay buffer, human corrections when needed, and 100 online trajectories per task.

Takeaway VeloGrad keeps the generative sampler intact. The learned velocity critic is a local value-gradient interface: cheap enough for actor updates, faithful enough to the remaining flow dynamics.

06 / ABLATION

The local interface is not interchangeable with BPTT or proxy Q.

Offline Cube-Triple isolates query-state construction, actor-update variants, and finite-radius sensitivity supervision.

Takeaway Replay-anchored continuous-time queries, a learned velocity critic, and finite-radius derivative matching are all load-bearing. Per-step training cost matches QAM; sensitivity adds 16.2% overhead.
